# Regolith Paved Airstrip "Runway" — Image Generation Prompts

## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "RA-1 Runway," a certified regolith landing facility,
drawn as a vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background, fine white and cyan
linework, faint drafting grid. Across the top, a bordered banner reads "⚠ THE NAME IS INHERITED
AND WRONG — no air = no lift; nothing will ever roll down this and take off; arrivals are
vertical, under thrust, on legs." Main view is a concentric site plan, captioned "site plan —
concentric, because the hazard is radial": at the centre a small circle labeled "touchdown
circle" with a cross-shaped landing marking and a solid centre dot; around it a solid bold
circle filled slightly darker labeled "bound (sintered) area"; around that a wide dashed circle
labeled "exclusion radius — nothing unprotected inside." Four arrows radiate outward from the
touchdown point past the bound area, labeled "plume ejecta — flat, fast, no air to stop it."
Four small solid triangles sit at the corners of the plan labeled "survey monument," connected
to each other by faint dashed sight-lines forming a square with diagonals, captioned "local
geodetic net — there is no GPS here." To one side, a rectangle labeled "apron" with a short
double-line track leading off labeled "→ paved route." A dimension line from centre to bound
edge is captioned "set by largest plume." Inset panel top-right titled "what is actually
specified" listing flatness tolerance, slope limit, bearing capacity, bound-area radius,
exclusion radius, markings/reflectors and re-survey interval, each with an arrow to what sets it
(gear stroke, centre of mass, crust thickness, largest plume, largest vehicle, arriving sensors,
degradation rate), captioned "every number belongs to a vehicle, not to the ground." Magnified
detail circle showing a cross-section: a loaded footpad resting on a thin hatched "crust" layer
above scattered dots representing "uncompacted soil beneath," captioned "a pad that punches
through is worse than dust," labeled "detail A — the crust is thin." Two bordered panels at the
bottom: "! THIS HAZARD IS RECORD, NOT ESTIMATE — Apollo 12 landed near Surveyor III; parts
returned to Earth showed erosion and pitting from the LM plume," and "! THE AWKWARD CIRCULARITY
— the spec cannot be written until the fleet exists; the fleet cannot be certified until the
spec exists." Numbered callout circles, a specification index panel, and a title block reading
"RA-1 RUNWAY — THE STANDARD, NOT THE MACHINE." Monospaced drafting typography, flat orthographic
projection, no shading, no photorealism.
```

## Most-buildable real product prompt (Phase 1: a single certified landing pad on the Moon)

```
Photorealistic render of a certified lunar landing pad viewed from a low angle across the
surface: a broad circular apron of dark, smooth, glassy sintered regolith, visibly distinct
from the loose pale-grey powdery ground beyond its sharply defined edge. Painted or inlaid on
the pad surface is a high-contrast cross-shaped touchdown marking with a bright centre target
and a stencilled pad identifier "PAD 01 — RATED 12 t." Around the pad's perimeter stand short
survey monuments: squat tripod markers topped with corner-cube retroreflectors catching the
sunlight, plus low posts carrying radar reflectors and a small placard. A hardened marker at the
edge reads "EXCLUSION RADIUS." In the mid-distance a cargo lander rests on four splayed legs at
the pad centre, its footpads flat on the fused crust. A short paved sintered track runs from the
apron toward a habitat module on the horizon. Fine detail: fine dust dulling the pad edges, a
faint scour pattern radiating from the centre, bootprints and rover tracks only on the unbound
ground outside the pad, hazard striping on the monuments. Black star-dusted sky, harsh low
sunlight throwing long knife-edged shadows. Shot at a three-quarter angle emphasising the
contrast between fused pad and loose regolith, hyper-detailed, aerospace documentary photography
style, realistic and grounded.
```
