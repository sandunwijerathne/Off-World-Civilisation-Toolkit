# Orbital Tug Pool "Mule" — Image Generation Prompts

## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "OT-1 Mule," a shared reusable orbital tug fleet, drawn
as a vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background, fine white and cyan linework,
faint drafting grid. Main view is an orbital transfer diagram: a central planetary body at left
of centre drawn as a banded circle, ringed by two dashed concentric orbits labeled "low orbit"
and "high orbit." A dashed transfer ellipse runs between them, touching the low orbit at a
marked point labeled "burn 1 — raise" and the high orbit at a point labeled "burn 2 —
circularise," captioned "transfer ellipse." On the transfer path sits a small blocky tug with a
rectangular payload attached at one end and thruster marks at the other, labeled "tug +
payload." A dimension line spans the two burn points labeled "Δv — the currency. Not distance."
On the low orbit sits a small twin-tank module labeled "depot — refuel, don't discard." A
bordered panel titled "the pool — dispatched, not owned" lists five small tug icons with
statuses: "chem · en route," "chem · refuelling," "elec · spiralling out," "elec · loading,"
"chem · inbound, empty?", captioned "fleet sized to aggregate demand — not to everyone's peak."
Inset panel top-right titled "one pool, two temperaments" showing a short thick arc labeled
"chemical — high thrust, short burn / hours–days / lots of propellant" above a long outward
spiral labeled "electric — tiny thrust, continuous / weeks–months / little propellant, big
power supply," captioned "neither dominates." Magnified detail circle showing two identical
halves of an androgynous docking interface meeting face to face with aligned connector lines,
captioned "one interface, both ends — structure · propellant · power · data — every payload
fits every mover," labeled "detail A — the common dock." A bordered panel reads "! REFUELLING
IS THE PIVOT: cryogenic propellant boils off and will not settle in a tank without gravity; a
tug that cannot be refuelled is just an expensive single-use stage with extra steps;
standardisation needs agreement before hardware — and agreement is slower than engineering."
Numbered callout circles, a parts list panel, and a title block reading "OT-1 MULE — GENERAL
ARRANGEMENT." Monospaced drafting typography, flat orthographic projection, no shading, no
photorealism.
```

## Most-buildable real product prompt (Phase 1: a single reusable tug docking at a depot)

```
Photorealistic render of a reusable orbital tug closing on a propellant depot in high orbit: a
compact utilitarian spacecraft with no wings, no heat shield and no landing legs — a squat
cylindrical core wrapped in gold and white thermal blanketing, flanked by two large spherical
propellant tanks, a cluster of small attitude thrusters at the corners, and a single main engine
bell at the rear. At its forward end, an androgynous docking ring with alignment guides and
fluid-transfer couplings is centimetres from mating with the matching ring on the depot, a larger
drum-shaped module with multiple tank spheres and a long radiator panel. A boxy cargo container
is clamped to the tug's mid-section on standardised hardpoints. Fine engineering detail: fluid
lines and quick-disconnects, docking cameras and laser rangefinder, grapple fixtures, tidy cable
runs, scuffed blanketing and micrometeoroid pitting from previous trips, stencilled markings
"TUG 04" and "PROPELLANT — LOX/LH2." Behind them the curve of a grey cratered moon and the black
star-dusted sky; hard low sunlight throwing long shadows across both vehicles, with a rim of
light along the docking ring. Shot at a three-quarter angle capturing the moment before contact,
hyper-detailed, aerospace documentary photography style, realistic and grounded.
```
