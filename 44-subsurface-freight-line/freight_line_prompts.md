# Subsurface Freight Line "Cuniculus" — Image Generation Prompts

## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "SF-1 Cuniculus," a lava-tube freight railway, drawn as a
vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background, fine white and cyan linework, faint
drafting grid. Main view is a long geological cross-section. Along the top runs a hatched
"surface" line, broken by two openings; arrows strike it from above labeled "cosmic rays ·
micrometeorites · 14-day thermal swing." Beneath the surface lies a thick banded layer labeled
"tens of metres of rock — the roof is the whole point." The two surface openings are skylight
shafts angling down into a large irregular cavern below, each labeled "skylight / station" with
a vertical hoist line and a small hoist car, labeled "hoist." The cavern is drawn with an uneven
wavy roof and floor, labeled "lava tube — a tunnel nobody bored, near-constant temperature, no
dust, no impacts." Scattered rounded blocks lie on the cavern floor at both ends, labeled
"breakdown blocks — proof the roof spalls." Between the two stations, a double-line railway track
with regular sleeper ticks runs along a cleared section of floor, labeled "cleared + graded floor
· powered track." On the track sits a short train: a locomotive unit and two flatbed wagons with
small wheels, labeled "unpressurised flatbeds · sealed containers," with a direction arrow ahead
of it. Inset panel titled "inertia stays · grip vanishes" showing two identical wagons on rails
side by side: the left with a large heavy downward arrow labeled "Earth: full weight, grip =
plenty ✓", the right with a tiny downward arrow labeled "Moon: 1/6 weight, grip = 1/6 ✗", joined
by a dashed line labeled "same mass," captioned "same force needed to accelerate, one sixth the
grip to supply it — so wheels support and guide, linear motors propel and brake." Magnified
detail circle showing a wagon underside with a row of motor coils above a flat "reaction rail —
pushed, not gripped," with support wheels either side on a running rail, captioned "wheels carry
weight only — no traction asked," labeled "detail A — linear drive." A bordered panel reads "!
THE ROUTE IS NOT YOURS TO CHOOSE: a tube goes where the lava went — it owes you nothing;
meanders · changes gradient · narrows · roof spalls; the tube gives the expensive half of a
tunnel — the hole — and none of the cheap half that makes it a railway." Numbered callout
circles, a parts list panel, and a title block. Monospaced drafting typography, flat orthographic
projection, no shading, no photorealism.
```

## Most-buildable real product prompt (Phase 1: freight train inside a lunar lava tube)

```
Photorealistic render of an automated freight train running through a lunar lava tube: a low
flatbed rail vehicle carrying two sealed grey cargo containers, moving along a pair of steel
rails laid on a cleared and graded floor of dark basalt. Between the rails runs a flat aluminium
reaction rail for linear-motor propulsion, with coil housings visible beneath the wagon chassis.
The vehicle carries its own work lights, throwing hard white beams forward and picking out the
rough, ropy, frozen-lava texture of the tube walls and the curved ceiling far above; beyond the
beams the cave falls into total blackness. Large fallen breakdown blocks sit on the untouched
floor beyond the graded track bed. Fine engineering detail: cable trays and power conduit clipped
along one wall, small reflective survey markers and distance placards at intervals, a monitoring
sensor package bolted to the rock, fine grey dust on the rail webs, stencilled marking "FREIGHT
04." In the far distance a shaft of pale sunlight falls through a skylight opening onto the cave
floor, illuminating hanging dust and a station platform with a hoist frame. Shot from a low
three-quarter angle just ahead of the train, deep contrast between lit rock and darkness,
hyper-detailed, industrial documentary photography style, realistic and grounded.
```
