# The Deep-Down Cryobot

*Category: AI Robots · Off-World Civilisation Toolkit*

Somewhere under Europa's ice, there is an ocean holding twice the water of all Earth's seas, warmed by tidal flexing, in contact with a rocky floor — every ingredient of life's recipe, sealed under kilometres of ice for four billion years. The Cryobot is the machine built to knock: a nuclear-heated cylinder that melts its way down through the shell over months, paying out a fibre-optic tether, then releases an autonomous swimmer into the dark.

That swimmer is the loneliest AI ever proposed. Jupiter is 45+ light-minutes away; no human can drive it. It must choose its own survey routes, recognise 'interesting' — possibly meaning *alive* — with no examples to train on, manage its own power and risks, and decide what evidence justifies spending its limited life to chase. NASA and JPL have run cryobot concept studies for years, and Antarctic melt probes descend through ice sheets today.

**Status: the mission our grandchildren will watch live; the AI architecture is worth designing now.**
