# Self-Healing Pressure Skin "Coagulant" — Image Generation Prompts

## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "SH-1 Coagulant," a self-healing spacecraft
hull skin, drawn as a vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background,
fine white and cyan linework, faint drafting grid. Main view is a large horizontal
cross-section of a three-layer hull membrane: a top hatched band labeled "outer
solid wall," a middle band with wavy lines labeled "reactive liquid healing agent,"
and a bottom hatched band labeled "inner solid wall (pressure side)." Above the
skin is labeled "space (vacuum) — micrometeoroid field"; below it "cabin
(pressurised air)." At the left, a small round micrometeoroid with a white arrow
drives down toward the skin, labeled "micrometeoroid (mm-scale)." In the middle, a
puncture channel runs vertically through all three layers; cyan arrows show cabin
air escaping upward out of the hole labeled "escaping air drags sealant up," curved
arrows show the liquid layer being pulled into the channel, and a dark solidified
shape fills the channel labeled "hardens on exposure, plug set in under 1 second."
Inset panel top-right titled "heal sequence (passive)" showing three small
three-layer strips in a row: "1 intact," "2 pierced" (with a dashed hole), "3
sealed" (with a plug), captioned "no sensor, no valve, no crew action — the leak
drives its own repair — seals airtightness, not strength, buys time." Magnified
detail circle showing a 1918 aircraft fuel tank wall with a rubber layer and a
bullet, captioned "same idea, 100+ years proven." A bordered panel reads "! HONEST
LIMIT: seals mm punctures, not large breaches; a third line behind shielding and
structure." Numbered callout circles, a parts list panel, and a title block reading
"SH-1 COAGULANT — GENERAL ARRANGEMENT." Monospaced drafting typography, flat
orthographic projection, no shading, no photorealism.
```

```
Photorealistic close-up render of a flat rectangular self-healing hull patch panel
mounted on the interior wall of a space station module. The panel is a layered
composite about the size of a laptop, with a slightly soft, matte outer face and a
faintly translucent edge revealing a thin fluid layer sandwiched inside between two
firmer skins. In the centre, a tiny sealed puncture is visible as a small dark

white cabin lighting. Shot as a crisp macro photograph with shallow depth of field,
hyper-detailed, aerospace documentary photography style, realistic and grounded.
```
