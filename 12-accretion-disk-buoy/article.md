# The Accretion-Disk Weather Buoy

*Category: Black Holes & Extreme Physics · Off-World Civilisation Toolkit*

The most violent weather in the universe surrounds black holes: accretion disks where plasma heats to millions of degrees, magnetic fields snap and reconnect with the energy of stellar flares, and matter spirals through physics no laboratory can recreate. The Weather Buoy is a swarm of radiation-hardened probes deployed on elongated orbits that repeatedly skim the outer disk environment of a quiet stellar-mass black hole, sampling plasma density, magnetic topology, and relativistic particle flux — then relaying the data out through a chain of mother-ships parked at safe distance.

Each buoy is built like a deep-sea instrument: a tungsten-shielded core, sacrificial outer layers, and instruments that expect to die in order. The science payoff is fundamental — accretion physics governs everything from quasars to the X-ray binaries in our own galaxy, and we currently study it only from thousands of light-years away.

**Status: requires interstellar travel first; the instrument design, though, could start today.**
