# The Directed-Energy Debris Broom

*Category: Defense Systems · Off-World Civilisation Toolkit*

Orbit is filling with shrapnel — millions of fragments too small to track and catch, each moving ten times faster than a rifle bullet. You cannot grab them. But you can push them with light. The Broom is a network of ground stations and orbital platforms that fire precisely-timed laser pulses at catalogued debris; each pulse ablates a whisker of material off the fragment's surface, and the recoil acts as a tiny rocket burn, nudging the fragment's perigee down into the atmosphere where it burns up harmlessly. No contact, no capture mechanism, one broom serving thousands of targets per year.

Laser ablation for debris removal is under active study at JAXA, ESA, and multiple national labs; the tracking precision required is the hard part and improves annually. The dual-use shadow is obvious — a laser that nudges debris can dazzle a satellite — which is why the Broom belongs under international operation.

**Status: technically within a decade; institutionally, the harder build.**
