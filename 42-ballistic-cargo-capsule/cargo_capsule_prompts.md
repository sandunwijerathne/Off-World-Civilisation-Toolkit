# Ballistic Cargo Capsule "Trebuchet's Child" — Image Generation Prompts

## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "BC-1 Trebuchet's Child," a ballistic cargo re-entry
capsule, drawn as a vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background, fine white and
cyan linework, faint drafting grid. Main view is an entry-corridor diagram: a broad curved
planetary surface arc across the lower portion labeled "surface," with two dashed arcs above it
marking the atmosphere, labeled "atmosphere — the free brake, costs nothing, never runs out." At
upper left a small tug releases a capsule, labeled "released by tug — aim is set here, capsule
cannot steer." Three trajectories descend from that release point: a dashed one that grazes the
atmosphere and curves back out labeled "too shallow → skips back out"; a bold solid one through
the middle labeled "the corridor"; and a dashed steep one ending in an X labeled "too steep →
heating + g exceed limits." On the correct path sits a small blunt capsule with a curved
detached arc drawn ahead of it labeled "detached bow shock." Lower down, a deployed parachute
canopy with shroud lines and a small capsule beneath it, labeled "chute / parafoil, then
touchdown," above a dashed flat ellipse labeled "lands in an ellipse, not at a point." Inset
panel top-right titled "why blunt beats sharp": a sharp dart shape with its shock clinging to
the body labeled "shock clings — heat goes into the vehicle ✗," and below it a rounded blunt
shape with a detached curved shock standing well in front labeled "shock stands off — heat goes
into the air ✓," credited "Allen & Eggers, 1958." Magnified detail circle showing the capsule in
section: a curved "blunt heat shield" at the base, a tapered "cargo" shell above it, and a
"chute bay" on top, captioned "no engine · no wings · no legs," labeled "detail A — the whole
vehicle." Two bordered panels at the bottom: "where the trick works" listing Earth, Titan, and
Moon/asteroids with verdicts; and "! MARS — A PARTIAL REFUND, NOT A FREE ONE" explaining that
~1% density is thick enough to burn a vehicle but too thin to stop one. Numbered callout
circles, a parts list panel, and a title block. Monospaced drafting typography, flat orthographic
projection, no shading, no photorealism.
```

## Most-buildable real product prompt (Phase 1: cargo capsule under parafoil after entry)

```
Photorealistic render of a blunt-bodied cargo capsule descending under a steerable ram-air
parafoil: a squat truncated-cone vehicle roughly three metres across, its wide underside a
charred dark-grey ablative heat shield visibly scorched and streaked from entry, the upper shell
clad in scuffed white and grey thermal blanketing with stencilled markings "CARGO 17" and
handling arrows. A rectangular grey parafoil canopy spreads above it on taut risers, with small
control-line actuators visible at the attachment points. Fine engineering detail: recovery
beacon antennas, lifting lugs, a standardised handling interface ring on the upper deck matching
the tug's docking pattern, crush-core skirt around the base, inspection decals, and faint heat
discolouration fading up the sides. Setting: a few hundred metres above a wide flat desert
recovery zone at low sun, long shadows across the ground, a recovery vehicle and marked landing
target small in the distance below, thin high cloud. Shot from slightly above and to the side,
capturing both canopy and scorched shield, hyper-detailed, aerospace documentary photography
style, realistic and grounded.
```
