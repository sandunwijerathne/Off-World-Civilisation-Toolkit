# Isotope Sorting Column "Aston" — Image Generation Prompts

> **Note on scope.** This concept is dual-use and is treated throughout at the level of
> textbook principle only. Following the same convention used for the weaponry concepts in
> Volume I, there is **no "most-buildable product" prompt** here — no plant, no hardware, no
> apparatus. The second prompt below is a museum / policy illustration instead, because the
> substance of this concept is its governance, not its engineering.

## Blueprint prompt (principle sheet)

```
Technical engineering blueprint titled "isotope sorting column — principle only," drawn as a
vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background, fine white and cyan linework,
faint drafting grid. Across the top is a bordered warning banner reading "NOTE ON SCOPE —
THIS SHEET IS DELIBERATELY INCOMPLETE: dual-use technology, textbook principle only, no plant
layout, no staging, no operating parameters — by intent." The main area shows a physics
diagram, not a machine: two plain circles side by side labeled "same protons / isotope A
(lighter)" and "+ extra neutrons / isotope B (heavier)," captioned "chemically nearly
identical — no chemistry can sort them." To the right, an arrow of "charged atoms" enters a
dashed rectangle of small dots labeled "magnetic field," and two curved paths of different
radii emerge, labeled "lighter — tighter curve" and "heavier — wider curve," captioned "path
radius depends on mass → they arrive apart (the mass spectrograph — how isotopes were
discovered)." A bordered box reads "why it is hard — and why that matters: the mass difference
is minute, so any one pass sorts almost nothing; real separation is large, complex, and very
energy-hungry; that difficulty has been a proliferation barrier for 80 years." A second
bordered box lists honest difficulties about energy cost and contested helium-3 economics.
Inset panel top-right titled "the peaceful demand" listing helium-3, deuterium, stable
isotopes, and purified materials. Occupying the position where a magnified technical detail
would normally sit is a large bordered panel titled "THE GOVERNANCE PROBLEM," noting "(this
panel replaces the technical detail that would normally sit here)," contrasting Earth's
non-proliferation treaty, IAEA safeguards, and material accounting against the absence of any
tested off-world equivalent, and concluding "the institutions must come first — limiting
factor: not physics, trust." Bottom left panel reads "PRINCIPLE INDEX (no parts list — by
intent)." Title block reads "IS-1 ASTON — PRINCIPLE SHEET ONLY." Monospaced drafting
typography, flat orthographic projection, no shading, no photorealism, no depiction of any
apparatus.
```

## Illustrative prompt (museum / policy exhibit — deliberately not a machine)

```
Photorealistic render of a museum exhibit on the governance of dual-use technology in space: a
quiet gallery with a softly lit display case containing a historical brass-and-glass mass
spectrograph tube from the 1920s on a velvet mount, with a small engraved brass plaque. Behind
the case, a large wall panel is printed with the treaty text of the Outer Space Treaty of 1967
in clean typography, partially overlaid by a diagram of two curved particle paths of different
radii. A second wall panel headed "WHO VERIFIES?" shows a simple timeline of safeguards
institutions with a conspicuous gap after the year 1967. In the middle distance, two visitors
stand reading, one a scientist in plain clothes, both small against the panels. Fine detail:
polished floor with soft reflections, museum track lighting, a low bench, an accessibility
plaque, dust motes in the light beams. No industrial equipment, no plant, no apparatus other
than the antique instrument in its case. Contemplative, restrained, documentary-photography
mood, cool museum lighting with warm accents on the display case, hyper-detailed and realistic.
```
