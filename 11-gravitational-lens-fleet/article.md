# The Gravitational Lens Telescope Fleet

*Category: Black Holes & Extreme Physics · Off-World Civilisation Toolkit*

The largest telescope we will ever own is the Sun. Einstein's relativity bends light around massive bodies, and at 550+ astronomical units out, light from a distant star grazing the Sun converges into focus — an effective aperture wider than any mirror humanity could ever polish. Park a metre-class telescope on that focal line, block the Sun's glare with a coronagraph, and an exoplanet fifty light-years away resolves into a mapped world: continents, oceans, cloud bands, city lights if they exist.

This is a real, costed NASA-studied mission concept (the Solar Gravitational Lens mission), not fantasy — the physics was confirmed observationally in 1919. The challenge is the commute: 550 AU is more than ten times Voyager 1's distance, demanding solar-sail or nuclear-electric propulsion and a probe that stays alive for a 25-40 year cruise. Each target star needs its own focal-line probe, hence a fleet.

**Status: launchable within decades; results arrive a career later. Worth it.**
