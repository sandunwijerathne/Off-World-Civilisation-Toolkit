# Recycling Cascade "Ouroboros" — Image Generation Prompts

## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "RC-1 Ouroboros," a total materials reclamation
cascade, drawn as a vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background, fine white
and cyan linework, faint drafting grid. Main view is a vertical descending cascade on the
left: a hopper at top labeled "mixed refuse in (habitat · shops · greenhouse · plant)" feeding
down through five stacked rectangular stage boxes labeled in sequence "1 · sort / identify,"
"2 · reuse / repair," "3 · reform," "4 · break down," "5 · reduce (thermal)," each connected
by a downward arrow. From the right side of each stage a horizontal side-exit arrow leads to a
label: from stage 2 "→ back in service (all embodied work kept)," from stage 3 "→ feedstock
(material kept, shape lost)," from stage 4 "→ nutrients + gases (to algal loop · insect reactor
· crops)," from stage 5 "→ sorted elements (via vapour refinery)." Below the last stage a small
box reads "residue — the only true waste," captioned "design goal: make this as small as
possible." A long vertical arrow runs down the left margin labeled "energy cost rises · value
falls — so exit early." Inset panel top-right titled "why 'waste' is an Earth word" showing a
plain circle labeled "Earth: open" with an outward arrow captioned "'away' exists — huge
reservoirs absorb it," beside a circle enclosed in a dashed boundary labeled "habitat: closed"
with its outward arrow crossed out by an X, captioned "discarding is not disposal — it is
loss." Magnified detail circle showing a small central box labeled "settlement" ringed by three
curved arrows chasing each other in a loop, captioned "material goes round, not through" and
listing "organics → bio · metals → refinery · polymers → fabrication · water → life support,"
labeled "detail A — the return path of every concept." A bordered panel reads "! PERFECT
CLOSURE IS IMPOSSIBLE — SAY SO: every process loses a little: adhesion, dust, vapour, dilution;
so a trickle of new material is always needed to make up losses; composites resist sorting →
design for disassembly from the start; the gap between 95% and 100% is where the real
engineering lives." Numbered callout circles, a parts list panel, and a title block reading
"RC-1 OUROBOROS — GENERAL ARRANGEMENT" and "Volume II — final sheet." Monospaced drafting
typography, flat orthographic projection, no shading, no photorealism.
```

## Most-buildable real product prompt (Phase 1: repair and reclamation bay in a habitat)

```
Photorealistic render of a materials reclamation and repair bay inside a planetary habitat
module: a well-organised workshop wall with labelled sorting bins along the base marked
"ALUMINIUM," "POLYMER," "COMPOSITE," "ORGANIC," each partly filled with neatly separated
scrap. On the bench above, a technician's workspace with a half-disassembled pump laid out on
a mat with its parts numbered on tags, hand tools in a magnetic rack, a small benchtop
plastic-regrind machine with a hopper of shredded polymer flake, and a compact induction
crucible with a fresh cast ingot cooling beside it. A wall-mounted screen displays a simple
material-flow diagram with recovery percentages. Fine detail: a handheld material scanner on
its charging dock, a bin stencilled "RESIDUE — MINIMISE," dust extraction ducting, Velcro
patches, printed work instructions, and a placard reading "RECLAMATION BAY 01 — REPAIR FIRST."
Clean, orderly, purposeful; the aesthetic of a place where nothing is thrown away. Cool even
workshop lighting with a warm glow from the crucible. Shot at a three-quarter angle,
hyper-detailed, industrial documentary photography style, realistic and grounded.
```
