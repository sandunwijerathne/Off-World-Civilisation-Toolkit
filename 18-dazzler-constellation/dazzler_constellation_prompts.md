# Dazzler Constellation "DC-1" — Image Generation Prompts

> **Note on this concept's prompts.** As with the Rail Interceptor, this entry has
> **no "buildable product" prompt**. This is a counterspace concept presented as a
> comparative argument for restraint, not a proposal. Both prompts below are
> illustrative and conceptual only.

## Blueprint prompt (principle schematic only)

```
Technical blueprint-style schematic of a conceptual reversible sensor-denial
system, drawn as a vintage cyanotype diagram: deep prussian-blue background, fine
white and cyan linework, faint drafting grid. Clearly labeled "PRINCIPLE SCHEMATIC
ONLY — NOT AN ENGINEERING DRAWING." Left: a curved Earth-surface arc labeled "area
of interest." Above it, a target imaging satellite drawn as a boxy bus with two
gridded solar panels and a downward-facing sensor aperture, labeled "target
imaging satellite / optical sensor," with a dashed cone showing its field of view
down to Earth. Inside that cone, horizontal cyan wash-out lines are drawn labeled
"imagery washed out (temporary)." Right: three small satellites spaced along a
dashed orbital arc labeled "dazzler constellation"; a single cyan beam runs from
one of them to the target's sensor, labeled "light directed into sensor — sensor
overwhelmed, nothing damaged." Lower left: a bordered panel titled "effect is
reversible" showing two small boxes — one with wash-out lines labeled "on:
denied," an arrow labeled "stop," and one with a clear image icon labeled "off:
sees again" — with bullets "no damage, no fragments, no debris." Centre-bottom: a
prominent bordered panel titled "! LESS BAD IS NOT GOOD" listing: interference is
still a hostile act; attribution is hard; civil and safety satellites may be
affected; cheap effects may lower the threshold; reversible must be verified. A
principle index panel and a title block reading "DC-1 — PRINCIPLE SCHEMATIC
(CONCEPTUAL)" with the field "framing: least-bad option, argue for restraint."
Monospaced drafting typography, flat orthographic projection, no shading, no
photorealism.
```

## Illustrative concept-art prompt (policy illustration — not a buildable product)

```
Editorial illustration for a space-policy journal, photorealistic style, showing
two contrasting halves of one orbital scene above a curved blue Earth. On the left
half: the aftermath of a destructive anti-satellite strike — a shattered satellite
and an expanding cloud of hundreds of glinting metal fragments spreading along the
orbit, rendered cold and chaotic, with faint fragment trails wrapping the planet.
On the right half: an intact, undamaged satellite simply sitting in darkness with
its camera aperture briefly washed in soft light, everything else about it
untouched, calm and orderly, no fragments anywhere. A thin dividing line runs
between the halves. Muted, serious colour palette; documentary realism; strong
visual contrast between "permanent and shared" and "temporary and contained."
NOTE: a conceptual policy illustration about arms control — not a product render
and not an engineering depiction.
```
