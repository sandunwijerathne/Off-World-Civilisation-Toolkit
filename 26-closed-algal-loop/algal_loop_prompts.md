## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "AL-1 Chlorella," a closed-loop algal
life-support system, drawn as a vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue
background, fine white and cyan linework, faint drafting grid. Main view is a loop
diagram: on the left, a rounded rectangle labeled "crew cabin" containing a
stick-figure human labeled "respiration"; on the right, a large rounded tank labeled
"photobioreactor (microalgae)" dotted with small cell marks, with light rays coming
in from above labeled "light (sun / lamps)." A pipe curves from the cabin to the
reactor along the top labeled "exhaled CO2 →," and another curves back from the
reactor to the cabin along the bottom labeled "← regenerated O2," forming a closed
loop. A branch from the reactor leads to a small bin labeled "harvest → food," and an
inflow arrow at the bottom is labeled "nutrients + water (recycled)." Inset panel
top-right titled "proven lineage" with text: "BIOS-3 (USSR, 1970s-80s): crews
breathed on algae + plants; MELiSSA (ESA, decades): engineered closed micro-

detail circle showing a thin illuminated culture channel with light coming in from
above, small algae cells, a "CO2 in" arrow and an "O2 out" arrow, captioned "thin
culture = light reaches all" and "detail A — light + gas exchange." A bordered panel
reads "! LIVING SYSTEM = RESILIENT + FRAGILE: a culture can crash, keep a backup;
microgravity: gas and liquid don't separate; full closure is decades of engineering."
Numbered callout circles, a parts list panel, and a title block reading "AL-1
CHLORELLA — GENERAL ARRANGEMENT." Monospaced drafting typography, flat orthographic
projection, no shading, no photorealism.
```
