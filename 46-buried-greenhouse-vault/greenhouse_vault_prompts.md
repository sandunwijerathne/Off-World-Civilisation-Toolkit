# Buried Greenhouse Vault "Demeter" — Image Generation Prompts

## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "GV-1 Demeter," a buried greenhouse vault, drawn as a
vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background, fine white and cyan linework, faint
drafting grid. Across the top a bordered banner reads "⚠ A WINDOW IS A HOLE IN YOUR SHELTER — a
glazed dome is a radiation hole with crops in it; split the window's two jobs: collect above,
deliver below." Main view is a vertical section. Above a hatched "surface" line sits a small sun
with rays striking a parabolic "surface collector," captioned "exposure is free — nobody stands
here," and beside it a flat panelled "PV array" with an arrow labeled "electricity." Separate
arrows strike the surface from above labeled "radiation · impacts · 14-day swing." Directly
beneath the surface runs a thick banded layer with a dimension arrow marked "≈2 m regolith," and
labels "overburden ↓." A narrow twin-walled duct passes down through this layer from the
collector, labeled "small shielded penetration — not a window," with a light arrow inside it; a
dashed power cable runs down from the PV array. Below the overburden is a large rounded rectangle
labeled "pressurised vault — no transparent roof, no view of the sky," containing a row of small
rectangular luminaires with light cones beneath them labeled "diffusers + luminaires," and four
sets of three stacked horizontal growing racks with small leaf shapes on them, captioned "stacked
racks — buried volume is expensive per cubic metre." A small stick-figure labeled "shielded crew"
stands among them. Upward arrows inside the vault are labeled "pressure ↑" opposing downward
arrows labeled "overburden ↓." Inset panel titled "the overburden pays 3×" listing "1 · shield —
crew and seed stock; 2 · thermal mass — steady while the surface swings; 3 · ballast — holds the
roof against pressure," captioned "burial is not merely tolerable here — it is structurally
useful." Magnified detail circle showing a plant absorption curve: high at blue, dipping at
green, high at red, with an upward arrow at the green dip labeled "green is reflected — that is
why leaves look green," captioned "a lamp can send only the useful bands; a window sends
everything," labeled "detail A — spectrum." Two bordered panels at the bottom: "! THE LIGHT
BUDGET IS THE WHOLE PROBLEM — growing area is limited by available energy, not by excavation," and
"! WHAT BURIAL COSTS — resist the glazed surface dome; it photographs well and shields nothing."
Numbered callout circles, a parts list panel, and a title block. Monospaced drafting typography,
flat orthographic projection, no shading, no photorealism.
```

## Most-buildable real product prompt (Phase 1: lamp-lit buried salad vault)

```
Photorealistic render of the interior of a buried greenhouse vault on Mars: a windowless
pressurised chamber with pale composite wall panels and a visible arched structural ceiling, no
sky and no glazing anywhere. Rows of vertically stacked hydroponic growing racks fill the space,
each shelf densely planted with vivid green lettuce, herbs and leafy salad crops. Above every
shelf runs a slim LED light bar casting the characteristic magenta-pink glow of combined red and
blue plant lighting, saturating the whole chamber in pink-violet light while the leaves read
intensely green beneath it. A technician in soft work clothes inspects a tray mid-frame. Fine
engineering detail: nutrient feed lines and drip manifolds, condensate channels, a small
environmental control panel with humidity and CO2 readouts, root-zone sensors, a sealed inner
hatch with a viewport, cable trays, and a stencilled sign "GROW VAULT 01 — BAY 3." At the far end,
a narrow ceiling duct houses a bundle of optical fibres feeding a diffuser panel, glowing with
natural white daylight piped from the surface — a visible contrast against the magenta LEDs. Humid
air, faint condensation on cool surfaces. Shot down the length of the aisle at a slight angle,
hyper-detailed, agricultural-technology documentary photography style, realistic and grounded.
```
