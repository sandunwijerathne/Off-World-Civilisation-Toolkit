## Blueprint prompt

```
Technical engineering blueprint of the "TB-1 Hypocaust," a lunar thermal-mass battery, drawn as a
vintage cyanotype schematic: deep prussian-blue background, fine white and cyan linework, faint
drafting grid, with amber accents marking heat flow. Main view is a vertical section. Along the top
runs a hatched "surface" line; above it a small parabolic dish labeled "day: concentrator" sends a
bold amber arrow down into the ground. Below the surface, a large dashed-outline region speckled
with scattered dots is labeled "loose regolith jacket — insulates, costs nothing." Inside that
jacket sits a solid rectangular block drawn as coursed masonry, shaded darker, labeled "dense core —
sintered / cast regolith," with a continuous amber serpentine line winding back and forth through it
labeled "embedded heat pipes." A dashed amber arrow enters from the left labeled "waste heat — kiln ·
refinery." To the right stands a tall rounded rectangle labeled "habitat — water · crops · bearings
· people," with an amber curved arrow running from the core to it labeled "night: draw down." Below
the section reads "~1 m down, the monthly swing is already gone." Inset panel titled "regolith is a
blanket" showing a graph with temperature across the top and depth downward: two curves start far
apart at the surface labeled "night" and "noon" and converge with depth to a single vertical line
labeled "~constant," captioned "no air between grains → almost no conduction (Langseth et al.,
1976)." Magnified detail circle titled "same substance": on the left, loose round grains touching at
single points labeled "loose — point contacts, vacuum gaps"; an amber arrow; on the right, a solid
fused block with wavy internal lines labeled "fused — continuous path, heat moves," labeled "detail
A — one material, two states." Two bordered panels: "! HEAT WANTS TO STAY HEAT — supplying heat:
superb; supplying electricity: mediocre; it does not replace electrical storage, it removes the

change; the mass is free, the sintering energy is not." Numbered callout circles, a parts list
panel, and a title block. Monospaced drafting typography, flat orthographic projection, no shading,
no photorealism.
```

## Most-buildable real product prompt (Phase 1: survival heat store beside a lunar habitat)

```
Photorealistic render of a thermal storage installation beside a lunar habitat, shown in a
partially excavated cutaway during construction: a large rectangular mass of dark, dense sintered
regolith blocks laid in neat courses, with copper-coloured heat-pipe manifolds emerging from its
face and running in insulated conduit toward the habitat's hull a few metres away. The upper side
of the block is being covered by a thick heaped jacket of pale, loose grey regolith, spilling from
the blade of a small excavator rover; one end is still exposed, revealing the block structure and
the pipe headers. Fine engineering detail: temperature-sensor leads fanning out from the core into
a small data-logger box, frost-free insulated pipe couplings, hazard marker stakes, a stencilled
plate reading "HEAT STORE 01 — CORE TEMP" beside a small readout, fine grey dust on every surface.
Behind, a small tracking solar concentrator dish stands on the plain, its focus aimed at a
receiver feeding the store. Setting: lunar late afternoon, the sun very low on the horizon casting
extremely long sharp shadows, black star-dusted sky, the habitat's warm window lights beginning to
glow — the night approaching that the store exists to survive. Shot at a three-quarter angle,
hyper-detailed, aerospace documentary photography style, realistic and grounded.
```
